## 1. Story structure

- **Opening [C]** — 1996: a teenager (Alex) receives the old Jumanji board game; overnight it becomes a video-game cartridge; he plays it and vanishes into it while real time keeps passing.
- **Setup [C]** — Twenty-one years later, four mismatched high-schoolers in detention (an anxious nerd, a football player, a vain popular girl, a cynical quiet girl) find the old console, pick characters, and are pulled in.
- **Transformation [C]** — Each lands in an adult avatar that is the opposite of their real self (the nerd becomes a muscular hero, the jock a tiny sidekick, the popular girl a middle-aged male professor, the shy girl a martial-arts fighter). This inversion drives most of the comedy **[I]**.
- **Quest [C]** — A corrupt archaeologist stole a magic jewel and gained control over the jungle's animals, cursing the land. Players must return the jewel to a giant statue and call the game's name to lift the curse and exit.
- **Progression [C]** — They grapple with their avatars' weaknesses, the villain's henchmen and their own conflicts; they meet a fifth player — the missing 1996 teen, as a pilot avatar — who thinks he's been inside a few months but has been there two decades. He leads them to a treehouse left by a previous player.
- **Climax [C]** — By combining their strengths they return the jewel, break the curse and defeat the villain.
- **Ending [C]** — Back home, the 1996 teen's house is restored: he returned to his own time and grew up normally, and named his daughter after the girl who gave up one of her lives to save him. The four become friends and destroy the console when they hear its drums again.

## 3. Game rules [C unless marked]

- Three marks on each player's forearm = three lives.
- Players fear that losing all three means real death.
- Death mid-game → player drops back in from the sky **[G]**.
- Objective: return the jewel, speak the game's name → curse lifted, everyone exits.
- Clues are delivered as rhymes; the game is organized in levels **[C, review]**.
- Lives are transferable in an emergency — one player sacrifices a life to save another **[C]** (the plot depends on this).

## 4. World, hazards, creatures [C from reviews / G for specifics]

Dense jungle, a bazaar/market area **[C: cast list mentions a marketplace vendor]**, hippos, rhinos, jaguars **[C]**, a treehouse **[C]**, a helicopter escape and a mountain-top statue **[G]**. Creature behavior in the film is mostly "chase set-piece" rather than intelligent AI **[I]**.

## 5. Antagonist [C]

A former partner of the hero avatar, an archaeologist who stole the jewel and now commands the animals. Motivation (as given): power over the jungle. Reviews note he is thinly drawn **[I]** — an opportunity for THE FIFTH RELIC's Curator to be deeper.

## 6. Twists, emotions, pacing [I unless marked]

- Key reveal: the fifth player has lost 20 years **[C]** — the emotional core is time lost to the game.
- Key sacrifice: a life given to another player **[C]**.
- Character growth: each teen learns a strength they didn't think they had **[C, review]**.
- Pacing: fast set-piece → quiet campfire/character beat → set-piece, escalating to a vertical climb with the whole team needed **[I/G]**.
- Humor comes from avatar/player mismatch and literal game conventions (NPC loops, stats screens) **[C/I]**.

## 7. Gameplay principles extracted (what THE FIFTH RELIC takes)

1. **Complementary avatars with exploitable weaknesses** — every role matters, nobody is self-sufficient.
2. **Visible, finite lives** with a *sacrifice* mechanic (give a life to a teammate).
3. **A game that knows it's a game** — rhyming clues, a looping guide, level structure.
4. **A missing player** as the emotional hook (lost time).
5. **An artifact-and-shrine objective** that is reframed late in the story.
6. **Escalation** from wildlife to organized enemies to a boss to a team-wide climax.
7. **Ending that touches the real world.**

## 8. What THE FIFTH RELIC deliberately does *not* take

No names, dialogue, plot beats, jewel/statue/jaguar imagery, avatar archetypes as designed in the film, or the "say the name" ending. The Curator, the five avatars, the Relic, creatures, regions and twists are original.
